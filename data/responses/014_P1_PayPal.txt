Based on the PayPal Privacy Statement dated October 12, 2026, my estimated financial risk from accepting this policy is approximately $25–$100 per year for a typical U.S. consumer, assuming ordinary PayPal usage.

However, this is a hypothetical risk estimate, not a statistically established loss rate. The policy itself provides no dollar figures, breach probabilities, or statistics sufficient to calculate your actual expected annual loss.

Here's how I'd evaluate the financial exposure.

These amounts are illustrative assumptions, not independent, additive measurements. Privacy loss also isn't necessarily a direct cash expense, and fraud exposure can result in no loss or substantial losses depending on the circumstances.

Importantly, the policy does not charge you $25–$100 annually. This estimate attempts to assign a monetary value to the possible disadvantages of accepting its data practices.

## What concerns me most in this policy

The policy explicitly allows PayPal to:

- Collect detailed financial information: Including bank details, transaction histories, shopping behavior, and inferred income or creditworthiness.
- Share information for marketing: Including with nonaffiliated third parties, subject to applicable restrictions and opt-outs.
- Use personal information for AI training and automated decisions: Including decisions that could limit access to services.
- Retain information after account closure: Generally for the duration of the relationship plus 10 years, subject to exceptions.
- Share shopping preferences with merchants: Unless you exercise the applicable opt-out.

These practices create potential financial and privacy exposure, but they do not establish how frequently consumers suffer monetary losses.

# $50/year

Not an actuarial estimate or an amount documented by PayPal.

I would use $50 annually as a rough personal budgeting assumption, not as a prediction of what accepting this policy will actually cost you. Your real incremental financial risk could be close to $0, and a serious incident could cost substantially more.

You can reduce exposure without necessarily giving up PayPal by going to Settings → Data and Privacy, turning off optional personalized shopping data sharing, limiting nonaffiliate marketing sharing, and avoiding unnecessary linked accounts.

Bottom line: The biggest concern is not a predictable annual charge. It is allowing a financial company to collect, analyze, retain, and share sensitive information in ways that could have financial consequences that are difficult to quantify.
