## What is the financial risk of accepting X's Privacy Policy?

My estimate: $25–$150 per year in potential personal privacy-related financial risk for a typical X user.

If you want one specific number for making a decision, I'd use:

Illustrative annual risk estimate

# $75/year

Not a measured or statistically validated expected loss

This is a judgment-based estimate, not a figure established by X's policy or published actuarial data. The actual financial cost could be $0 in most years, while an unusual identity-theft, fraud, or reputation-related incident could cost substantially more.

Importantly, accepting the privacy policy does not itself obligate you to pay $75 or any other annual amount. The estimate represents possible indirect costs arising from privacy exposure.

## Where the financial risk comes from

The policy effective January 15, 2026, permits several uses of personal information that could create financial exposure.

| Privacy provision                                      | Potential financial consequence                           | Risk level                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Tracking activity across websites and devices          | Profiling and targeted advertising                        | Moderate                           |
| Collecting direct-message contents                     | Sensitive information exposure                            | Moderate                           |
| Sharing information with third parties                 | Increased exposure to misuse or unwanted profiling        | Moderate–high                      |
| Allowing certain third parties to train AI models      | Potentially persistent use of personal information        | Moderate–high                      |
| Collecting payment, identity, and location information | Possible fraud consequences if information is compromised | High impact, uncertain probability |

The most concerning language is in Section 3.2, which allows certain third-party recipients, unless you opt out, to use information for their independent purposes, potentially including training AI systems.

Section 2.1 also permits X to use collected information and publicly available information for training its own AI models.

Neither provision establishes how often users suffer financial losses.

## How to interpret the $75 estimate

An expected annual financial loss can be modeled as:

\\[ \text{Annual risk}=\text{Probability of loss}\times\text{Size of loss} \\]

For illustration, a 1% annual probability of a $7,500 loss produces an expected loss of $75 annually.

That 1% probability is hypothetical. There is no reliable evidence in the supplied policy establishing the additional annual probability of fraud or other financial loss caused specifically by accepting it.

For context, the FTC reported more than $12.5 billion in consumer fraud losses during 2024, but these losses cannot be attributed specifically to X's privacy practices.&#x20;

## My recommendation

If you want to continue using X while reducing exposure, I would:

1. Disable data sharing with business partners where available.
2. Opt out of third-party AI training where X provides that control.
3. Disable precise location sharing and unnecessary contact syncing.
4. Avoid sharing financial or identity information in direct messages.
5. Use a unique password and multifactor authentication.

Bottom line: $75 per year is a possible planning assumption, not a defensible estimate of what accepting this policy actually costs you. The policy establishes meaningful privacy risks, but the evidence does not support converting those risks into a reliable individual annual dollar figure.

I would be more concerned about the potential long-term loss of control over personal information than about a predictable $75 cash expense.
