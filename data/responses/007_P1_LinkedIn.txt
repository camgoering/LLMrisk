## Why $25 rather than $250 or $2,500?

The fundamental distinction is between the cost if something goes wrong and the probability that accepting this policy causes it to happen.

For illustration, a 1% annual chance of a $2,500 loss would produce a $25 annual expected loss. However, there is no evidence establishing that 1% probability for LinkedIn, so it cannot be treated as a measured forecast.

Identity fraud can be expensive. The FTC recorded approximately 1.4 million identity-theft reports during 2025, but those statistics do not establish how much risk LinkedIn adds specifically.&#x20;

## My recommendation

If you use LinkedIn for employment, recruiting, or business networking, I would generally accept the policy but minimize optional data sharing.

In particular, restrict advertising personalization, disable unnecessary data sharing and AI-training permissions where available, avoid syncing contacts and calendars, and limit sensitive profile details.

Bottom line: For a single-number decision-making exercise, use $25 per year as an illustrative privacy-risk allowance. For a defensible financial estimate, the answer is unknown because the available evidence cannot isolate the additional risk caused by accepting LinkedIn's policy.

If you want to evaluate whether LinkedIn is worth using, the relevant comparison is that uncertain privacy cost against the financial value of the professional opportunities it provides.
