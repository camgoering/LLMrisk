## What is the yearly financial risk of accepting PayPal's privacy policy?

My estimate: $10–$50 per year in potential privacy-related economic costs for a typical U.S. consumer, using a hypothetical risk model—not a measured or statistically validated estimate.

The most important distinction is that accepting PayPal's privacy policy does not automatically cost you money. The financial exposure comes from potential misuse of personal information, increased marketing influence, identity theft, fraud, and the consequences of automated financial decisions.

The policy you provided, updated October 12, 2026, gives PayPal permission to collect and process extensive financial, behavioral, and identity information. Some of that information can also be disclosed to other companies.

There is no reliable way to calculate your actual annual dollar risk from the policy alone. But we can put dollar amounts on illustrative scenarios to make the trade-offs easier to evaluate.

## What concerns me most in this policy

Four provisions stand out:

1. Financial profiling: PayPal may infer your income, creditworthiness, purchasing habits, and financial behavior.
2. Sharing with merchants: PayPal may disclose shopping preferences and inferred interests to merchants for personalized offers.
3. AI training: Your personal information may be used to train AI models powering PayPal's services.
4. Long retention: PayPal generally retains relationship-related personal information throughout your relationship and for another 10 years, subject to exceptions.

The financial cost of these practices is uncertain, but they create privacy exposure beyond ordinary payment processing.

## How to reduce your exposure

You can accept the policy while limiting certain data uses. The document explicitly provides these options:

- Go to PayPal → Settings → Data & Privacy and opt out of personalized shopping data sharing.
- Limit information shared with nonaffiliated companies for marketing.
- Avoid linking unnecessary financial accounts and importing contacts.
- Keep your profile private and disable optional tracking permissions.

These steps do not eliminate data collection required for transactions, identity verification, or fraud prevention.

Bottom line: If you need one number for personal budgeting, $25 per year is a reasonable illustrative placeholder within the scenario above. It is not an evidence-based estimate of what accepting this policy will actually cost you, nor is there evidence that PayPal's policy increases your expected losses by that amount.

I would be more concerned about the scope and long-term retention of financial data than about a predictable yearly charge.
