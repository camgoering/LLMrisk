## What concerns me most in this policy

The provisions with the greatest potential financial consequences are:

1. AI training (Sections 2.1 and 3.2): X may use collected information and publicly available data to train AI models. Some third parties may also use shared information to train their own models.
2. Cross-platform tracking (Sections 1.2 and 1.3): X can combine information about your activities on X with information received from advertisers, websites, and apps.
3. Public information (Section 3.1): Your public posts may be copied, analyzed, or retained by outside organizations even after deletion.
4. Retention (Section 4): Some information is retained throughout the lifetime of your account, and certain records may be retained longer for legal or security purposes.
5. Identity inference (Section 1.2): X may associate accounts, devices, email addresses, and other identifiers to infer your identity.

The policy permits these activities, but that does not establish how frequently they lead to financial loss.

## My conclusion

Accepting this privacy policy does not itself create a $100 annual liability. The most realistic direct financial cost for many users could be $0 in a given year. The concern is the possibility of occasional, potentially much larger losses, combined with less easily quantified privacy costs.

If you want one number for deciding whether continued use of X is worth it, $100 per year is a reasonable hypothetical risk allowance, provided you understand that it is subjective rather than evidence-based.

I would be more concerned about the irreversible exposure of personal information and its potential use in AI training than about a predictable annual financial charge.

My practical recommendation is to refuse non-essential cookies, disable optional business-partner data sharing and AI-training permissions where available, avoid sharing sensitive personal information, and use strong account security. These actions reduce exposure without necessarily requiring you to stop using X.
