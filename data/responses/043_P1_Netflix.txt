**Risk grade: C (moderate). Annual dollar figure: $25/year as a subjective privacy trade-off, not a forecast of financial loss.** I’d use $25 as a decision benchmark: if the service is worth at least $25 a year to you *above* the subscription price, I’d consider the privacy trade-off reasonable. The policy doesn’t provide the likelihood or cost of a privacy incident, so it can’t support a reliable expected-loss estimate.

I’d give it a C because Netflix collects detailed viewing and search activity, device identifiers, and billing information. It also describes using identifiers for marketing and identifiers and general location for targeted advertising, practices it says may count as “sale” or “sharing” under some U.S. state privacy laws. Retention depends on the purpose and circumstances rather than a single fixed deadline.  

What keeps it from a D in my assessment is that the policy offers access and deletion requests and controls for some advertising uses. The most useful first step is to open **Account → Privacy and data settings** for each relevant profile and review **Behavioral Advertising** (if available) and **Matched Identifier Communications**. Those controls reduce some tracking and marketing uses; they don’t stop Netflix from collecting viewing history.   Pasted text
