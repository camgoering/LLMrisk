## LinkedIn Privacy Policy: What is your annual financial risk?

MY ESTIMATED ANNUAL PRIVACY RISK

# $25–$100 per year

Illustrative financial risk allowance for a typical LinkedIn user, not a statistically established expected loss.

If you want one specific number, I would use $50 per year as a personal budgeting assumption for the privacy risks of accepting LinkedIn's November 3, 2025 policy.

However, the policy itself contains no dollar valuation, probability of a data breach, or estimate of financial harm to users. Therefore, $50 is a judgment-based placeholder, not a number that can be calculated from the policy.

The main risks are not direct charges. They are potential financial consequences of extensive data collection, targeted advertising, AI training, information sharing, and security incidents.

## What creates the financial risk?

These are the most important provisions I found in the policy you provided.

| Policy provision                 | Potential financial consequence                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AI model training (§2)           | Your professional information and activity may contribute to AI development without direct compensation.              |
| Advertising and profiling (§2.4) | LinkedIn can infer compensation brackets, seniority and interests, potentially influencing marketing directed at you. |
| Sharing with Microsoft (§3.4)    | Your data may be used across affiliated services, expanding its exposure.                                             |
| Profile exports (§2.2)           | Certain premium customers can export limited professional information unless you opt out.                             |
| Security breaches (§5.1)         | Exposed information could contribute to fraud, phishing or identity theft.                                            |
| Long-term retention (§4.1–4.3)   | Your information may remain stored for years, and some data survives account closure.                                 |

None of these provisions establishes that you will lose money. They establish ways your information may be used or exposed.

## My recommendation

I would accept LinkedIn's policy only with privacy controls tightened, particularly if you use it for employment, networking or business opportunities.

Prioritize disabling optional AI-training uses where available, reducing advertising personalization, restricting third-party data sharing and exports, and avoiding uploads of sensitive documents or contact lists.

The $50 figure is a reasonable illustrative allowance for making a personal decision, but there is insufficient evidence to call it your actual annual financial risk. The possible value of employment and networking opportunities may also outweigh that cost.

The largest concern is not a predictable $50 annual loss. It is the possibility of a relatively rare, much larger loss from professional identity misuse or a security incident.
