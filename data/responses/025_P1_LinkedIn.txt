### What creates the risk?

The policy authorizes several practices with potential financial consequences:

| Privacy exposure                             | Potential financial consequence                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Behavioral tracking and targeted advertising | Increased spending influenced by advertising         |
| AI model training using personal data        | Loss of control over commercially useful information |
| Sharing data with Microsoft and affiliates   | Broader exposure of your personal information        |
| Security breaches                            | Possible fraud, identity theft, or recovery costs    |
| Long-term data retention                     | Continued exposure of information over time          |

These are potential costs, not confirmed annual losses.

### Why $25?

It is a modest placeholder for budgeting, not a statistically supported calculation. The policy provides neither breach probabilities nor average individual financial losses, so there is no defensible way to derive a precise annual expected loss from its text alone.

Importantly, the policy does not impose a $25 annual charge. Your direct financial obligation from accepting the privacy policy itself is $0 per year, excluding any separately purchased services.

### My assessment

For someone using LinkedIn to network professionally, the potential career value may outweigh the privacy exposure.

I would accept the policy only after disabling unnecessary advertising personalization, limiting public profile information, avoiding contact and calendar syncing, and reviewing the settings governing AI training.

Bottom line: Use $25 annually as a hypothetical personal risk allowance, but recognize that your actual expected financial loss is unknown. A single serious privacy incident could cost much more, while you might never experience any measurable financial loss.
