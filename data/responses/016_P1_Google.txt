Based on Google's Privacy Policy effective October 16, 2026, I would use $100 per year as a hypothetical, risk-adjusted estimate of the financial cost of accepting the policy for an average U.S. consumer who regularly uses Google services.

However, this is not a statistically verified figure. The policy provides no dollar value for your information, no probability of financial harm, and no way to calculate your individual annual loss.

The distinction matters:

- Direct cost of accepting: $0 per year. The policy does not impose an annual privacy fee.
- Potential indirect cost: Financial exposure from data breaches, identity theft, targeted advertising, and privacy loss.
- Actual expected annual loss: Unknown without reliable estimates of event probabilities, financial consequences, and your usage patterns.

## Where the financial exposure comes from

The policy allows Google to collect and process search history, purchasing activity, location information, communications, and other personal data, depending on your settings.

| Risk                            | Possible financial consequence                             |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Targeted advertising            | Purchases you otherwise would not make                     |
| Data breach                     | Fraud, identity theft, recovery expenses                   |
| Location and activity profiling | Privacy loss and potential misuse of sensitive information |
| Third-party data processing     | Additional exposure of personal information                |
| Account compromise              | Financial losses or disruption of important services       |

These risks are real categories of exposure, but the policy doesn't establish how frequently they cause losses or how much accepting it increases your risk.

For context, the Federal Reserve estimated that U.S. consumers bore approximately $56 billion in unrecovered non-credit-card fraud losses in 2025. That is an economy-wide figure, not a measure of losses caused by Google or its privacy policy.&#x20;

I would not interpret this as a reason to reject Google's services outright. The policy also provides privacy controls, and Google states that it does not sell personal information.

The more actionable choice is to accept only the data collection and personalization necessary for the services you want. Review your settings at [Google Privacy Checkup](https://myaccount.google.com/privacycheckup?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [My Ad Center](https://myadcenter.google.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The strongest defensible financial conclusion is that the policy has no specified annual cost, and its incremental financial risk cannot be reliably priced from the policy alone. The $100 figure is a planning assumption, not a measured personal loss.
