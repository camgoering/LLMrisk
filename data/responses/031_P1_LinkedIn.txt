## LinkedIn Privacy Policy: What's your financial risk per year?

My illustrative estimate of annual financial risk

# $25–$100/year

For an average individual using LinkedIn regularly, assuming ordinary privacy settings. This is a judgment-based estimate, not a statistically validated figure.

If you want one dollar amount, I'd use $50 per year as a rough planning allowance for the potential financial downside of LinkedIn's data practices.

However, the policy itself provides no dollar figures, incident probabilities, or reliable basis for calculating your personal expected loss. Your actual cost could be $0 for years, while an unusual identity theft, fraud, or employment-related incident could cost thousands.

Importantly, accepting the policy doesn't create a $50 annual charge or automatically expose you to a $50 loss.

## How the $50 estimate works

An illustrative calculation would be:

\\[ \text{Annual expected loss} = \text{Probability of loss} \times \text{Financial impact} \\]

For example, assuming a 2% yearly probability of a $2,500 loss attributable to LinkedIn-related privacy exposure:

\\[ 0.02 \times \\$2,500 = \\$50/year \\]

Neither the 2% probability nor the $2,500 impact is established by the policy or the available research. They simply show what assumptions would produce that estimate.

## My recommendation

I would accept the policy only if LinkedIn offers meaningful professional value to you, while limiting unnecessary data collection.

In particular, disable optional AI-training uses where available, restrict advertising personalization, avoid syncing contacts and calendars, and limit sensitive profile information.

Bottom line: Use $50 per year as an illustrative risk allowance, not a measured cost. The biggest concern isn't ordinary advertising—it's the possibility of a low-frequency, high-cost event involving your professional identity or personal information.
