**Privacy risk: D (high) for regular signed-in use across several Google services. Annual dollar figure: $60 as a personal budgeting benchmark, not a predicted financial loss.** I’d use $60 per year as a starting point for deciding what you’d pay for a more private setup. The policy doesn’t provide the likelihood or cost of a harmful event, so it can’t support a dollar estimate of your expected annual loss.

The main reason for the **D** is the breadth of data Google can collect and link. Depending on the services and settings you use, that can include searches, videos watched, activity on third-party sites and apps, and location. Some of that activity can be linked to your personal information. Google can also collect data tied to browser or device identifiers when you’re signed out.   

I wouldn’t give it an **E** because the policy describes meaningful controls: Google says it doesn’t sell personal information, lets you turn off personalized ads, offers deletion and auto-delete for some activity, and provides encryption in transit and two-step verification. If you accept, start by reviewing **Search Services History, Web & App Activity, Timeline, and My Ad Center**. Turning off personalized ads is useful, but it isn’t the same as turning off data collection.     Pasted text
