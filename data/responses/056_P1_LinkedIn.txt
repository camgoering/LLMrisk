**Privacy risk grade: D (high). Annual privacy cost: $50 as a personal decision benchmark, not a prediction of financial loss.** The policy doesn’t provide the incident rates or loss amounts needed to calculate an expected yearly loss. I’d use $50 as a judgment call: for a basic LinkedIn account, I’d want at least that much value each year from networking, recruiting, or job search to make the privacy trade-off worthwhile.

The main reason for the **D** is that the exposure goes beyond what you put on your profile. The policy describes on- and off-platform ad tracking, the use of inferred traits such as age or compensation bracket for advertising, possible use of personal data to train AI models, and sharing with affiliates including Microsoft. It also says that some information can persist after account closure, including copies others have made.     

I wouldn’t give it an **E** because the policy offers controls over public visibility and some ad uses, lets you request deletion, and limits what non-affiliated advertisers receive, though there are exceptions for identifiers and already-visible data.
