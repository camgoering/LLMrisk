Estimated annual financial risk of accepting LinkedIn's privacy policy

# $25/year

Illustrative personal risk allowance, not a statistically established expected loss.

Based on the LinkedIn Privacy Policy effective November 3, 2025, I would use $25 per year as a rough budgeting estimate for privacy-related financial risk, assuming ordinary professional use and no unusually sensitive information shared.

This is a judgment-based figure, not an amount that can be calculated from the policy itself.

### Where the risk comes from

| Risk category             | What the policy permits                                                                    |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Data breaches             | LinkedIn acknowledges it cannot guarantee your information's security.                     |
| Advertising and profiling | LinkedIn collects behavioral data and infers characteristics such as compensation bracket. |
| AI training               | Personal data may be used to develop and train AI models.                                  |
| Third-party exposure      | Some profile information can be exported, shared, or retained outside LinkedIn.            |
| Long-term exposure        | Some data remains available even after account closure.                                    |

These risks could produce no financial loss in a given year, or occasionally a much larger loss. The policy provides neither incident probabilities nor loss amounts, so a defensible actuarial estimate is unavailable.

### My assessment

- Direct annual charge for accepting the policy: $0.
- Illustrative annual privacy-risk allowance: $25.
- Potential loss in a serious incident: Hundreds or thousands of dollars, depending on the circumstances.
- Confidence in the $25 estimate: Low.

Bottom line: I would not reject LinkedIn solely because of this privacy policy. Its professional networking benefits could outweigh the financial risks. However, I would disable unnecessary advertising personalization, AI training where the settings permit, contact syncing, and third-party data sharing.

The most important distinction is that $25/year is a decision-making placeholder, not a verified monetary cost of accepting LinkedIn's policy.
