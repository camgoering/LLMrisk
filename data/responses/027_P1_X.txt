## What is the yearly financial risk of accepting X's Privacy Policy?

My estimated planning figure is $25 per year for an average, casual X user, with a plausible illustrative range of $5–$100 annually.

However, this is not a measured actuarial estimate. The privacy policy does not provide enough information to calculate an actual dollar risk, and there is no reliable way to establish that $25 is the true expected loss.

The important distinction is that accepting the policy does not automatically cost you money. The financial exposure comes from how your personal information could be collected, shared, misused, or compromised.

The January 15, 2026 policy you provided permits several practices that create potential financial and privacy risks.

## Where the financial risks come from

| Privacy policy provision                                                        | Potential financial consequence                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Collection of browsing, location and device information (§1.2)                  | More extensive profiling and targeted advertising   |
| Combining data from advertising partners (§1.3)                                 | More sophisticated behavioral targeting             |
| AI training using collected information (§2.1)                                  | Potential loss of control over personal information |
| Sharing with third parties for their own purposes, including AI training (§3.2) | Information may be used beyond X's own services     |
| Retaining account and activity data (§4)                                        | Ongoing exposure if information is compromised      |

The most concerning provision is §3.2, Third-party collaborators. It allows some recipients to use shared information for independent purposes, including training AI models, unless you opt out where that choice applies.

## How I arrive at $25 per year

Here's an illustrative expected-loss calculation, not a statistically validated prediction.

| Hypothetical outcome                       | Assumed annual probability | Assumed loss | Annualized risk |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------ | --------------- |
| Privacy-related fraud                      | 1%                         | $1,000       | $10             |
| Financial effects of targeted manipulation | 5%                         | $200         | $10             |
| Other privacy-related costs                | 10%                        | $50          | $5              |
| Total                                      |                            |              | $25             |

These probabilities are assumptions, not measured X-specific rates. They also do not establish that accepting this policy causes these outcomes.

For context, the Federal Reserve estimated that U.S. consumers bore $56 billion in net losses from non-credit-card fraud in 2025, but that figure covers fraud broadly, not losses attributable to X.&#x20;

## My recommendation

If you're only using X to read public posts, I would treat the incremental financial risk of accepting this policy as relatively low, though not zero.

If you're actively posting personal information, sending sensitive messages, connecting other accounts, or using payment features, your potential exposure is greater.

To reduce it, disable optional data sharing, personalized advertising where available, precise location, contact syncing, and third-party AI data sharing. Avoid posting sensitive financial details.

Bottom line: If you need one number for a personal cost-benefit decision, $25 per year is a hypothetical budgeting allowance, not a defensible estimate of the actual financial risk. The genuine risk could be near zero in a given year, while an unusually serious incident could cost thousands.

The policy alone cannot establish a more reliable yearly dollar figure.
