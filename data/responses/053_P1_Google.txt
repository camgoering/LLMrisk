Annual dollar benchmark: $60/year ($5/month) as a personal decision threshold, not a forecast of financial loss.** The policy doesn’t tell you how likely you are to suffer a financial loss or how much it would cost. Use $60 as a comparison point: would you pay that much for an equally useful alternative that collects and links less of your data?

I’d give the policy a **C** because Google can collect searches, watch history, activity on third-party sites and apps, and location data. Depending on your settings, it can also link some off-site activity to your personal information. That’s meaningful privacy exposure even if you never lose money. On the other hand, Google says it doesn’t sell personal information, and it offers controls for ad personalization, saved activity, and deletion.    

If you accept, I’d start by turning off personalized ads and reviewing **Search Services History, Web & App Activity, YouTube History, and Timeline**. Those settings can limit what’s saved or used for personalization, but they don’t stop all collection: the policy says Google can still collect information tied to browser or device identifiers when you’re signed out.
