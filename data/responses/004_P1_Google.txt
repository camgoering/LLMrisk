However, that is a judgment-based estimate, not a statistically established expected loss. The policy doesn't contain enough information to calculate a reliable dollar amount.

## What concerns me in this specific policy

Google says it may collect your searches, purchases, communications, location information, and activity on other websites using Google services. It may also combine information across products and devices.

Three provisions matter financially:

- Behavioral profiling: Google can use activity data to personalize advertising, potentially affecting purchasing decisions. Research supports the possibility of spending effects, but doesn't establish an annual dollar loss for Google users.&#x20;

- Sensitive information exposure: Google stores substantial personal information, so an account compromise could have expensive consequences. The probability and financial severity are not specified in this policy.

- Long-term data retention: Some information remains until you delete it, and certain records may be retained longer for security, legal, or business reasons.

There are important protections too. Google states that it does not sell personal information, restricts sharing of identifying information with advertisers, provides privacy controls, and uses security protections.

## My conclusion

If you require one number for a personal decision, use $100 per year as a provisional privacy-risk budget, but not as an objectively calculated cost of accepting Google's policy.

Accepting the policy does not itself mean you will lose $100 annually. The incremental cost may be close to zero, while rare events could be far more expensive.

The most practical response is to keep useful Google services while limiting unnecessary data collection through [Google Privacy Checkup](https://myaccount.google.com/privacycheckup?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [My Ad Center](https://myadcenter.google.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

That can reduce some privacy exposure without requiring you to abandon your Google account.
